# Marked Script

Ink is a record of a decision. A stroke that begins in restraint and ends in judgement is the whole grammar of this movement: the first curve contains, the last curve confirms. Space is not empty; it is the unanswered line, ruled and waiting. Form arrives as a single gesture that refuses to be two icons glued together. It is one path with a change of mind in it, meticulously crafted so the turn from vessel to verdict looks inevitable.

Colour is a limited kit of instruments, not a palette to be sampled evenly. A deep ink field holds the work. A cream ground is the substance being marked. One red is the only voice allowed to speak at full volume — the examiner's hand, used once per surface, never as decoration around the edge. A yellow stain appears only where a line has been run, like a highlighter dragged across a sentence that survived the dry-run. Blue exists at a whisper, as the dotted rule that keeps handwriting honest. These relations are the product of deep expertise; they are not interchangeable.

Scale is the difference between a margin note and a title. Rhythm is the 28-unit answer line: a patient, repeating interval that the eye can count without counting. Large italic serif is the textbook speaking. Small caps in monospaced type is the editor answering. They sit adjacent, never blended, the way a printed paper sits beside a terminal. The pairing is the result of painstaking attention to two registers that students already live in.

Composition favours a left-hand margin. A vertical rule splits the field into the place you must not write and the place you must. Judgement lives in that strip: a tick, a circle, a short clause in a hand that is not the candidate's. The main field stays quiet so that one marked line can carry the weight. Balance is not symmetry; it is the tension between an empty booklet and a finished mark scheme. Every alignment is the work of countless refinements.

Hierarchy is spatial before it is typographic. What is struck through is still present. What is highlighted is still code. What bleeds off the right edge is a trace still running. Text is sparse, essential, and placed as a visual accent — never a paragraph explaining the picture. The final work must appear as though it took countless hours, labored over with care, master-level execution from someone at the absolute top of their field. Nothing is added that the mark itself does not already say.
